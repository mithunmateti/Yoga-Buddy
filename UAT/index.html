Basic Asanas

Standing poses

Poses on your feet, in the same order as the Standing Poses chapter of the book Yoga Anatomy. They build balance, strong legs and good posture, and what you learn here helps every other pose. Many are done on one side: do the other side afterwards too.

Sitting poses

Calm poses on the floor, plus chair yoga for anyone who finds the floor hard. Put the camera low, at about the height of your chest when you sit.

Kneeling poses

Poses on your knees. Put a folded blanket under your knees if they feel the floor, and put the camera low.

Lying on your back

Poses lying face up (yoga books call them supine poses). Put the camera low, near the floor, and lie sideways to it.

Lying on your tummy

Poses lying face down (yoga books call them prone poses). They make your back strong. Put the camera low, near the floor, and lie sideways to it.

On your hands

Poses where your hands and arms hold you up (yoga books call them arm support poses). They make your arms and middle strong. Put the camera low and turn sideways to it.

Surya Namaskar & Flows

A flow is poses one after another, like a moving yoga lesson. Yoga Buddy's voice tells you what to do next and checks each pose with the camera.
